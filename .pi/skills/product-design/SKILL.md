# Physical Product Design

Connect user need, function, experience and lifecycle for physical product concepts.

## Selection boundary

Choose as primary when the unresolved question is which physical product concept,
functional experience or use scenario best serves the user. Develop the value,
functions and lifecycle together. When architecture, mechanisms, assembly or CMF
are the main acceptance criteria, use industrial realization knowledge as primary
and retain product framing as supporting knowledge if useful. A digital app is a
different professional task. Do not activate two competing primaries in one scope.

## Define the need

Identify users, context, desired outcome and constraints before assuming a product
format. Translate research into primary functions and supporting tasks. Model
preparation, operation, feedback, cleaning, storage and maintenance where relevant.

Explore architecture, controls, support, storage, form and material as distinct axes.
Use integration/separation, reversal or adjacent mechanisms only through a plausible
user benefit. Compare ergonomics, use, coherence, differentiation, assembly and
lifecycle/cost drivers; novelty alone is insufficient.

## Develop the relationships

Specify reach, grip, posture, human scale and accessibility. Connect controls,
states and affordances to functional blocks, part interfaces, service and assembly.
Resolve proportion, silhouette, CMF, tactility, wear, repair and transport together.
Record dimensions and technical performance as assumptions unless supported.

For new products extract principles and independently resolve form, rather than
recoloring a competitor. For depiction preserve identifying features; for redesign
separate retained equity from authorized changes. Studying a mechanism is distinct
from reusing its source photograph or mark.

## Communicate and validate

Choose views for actual questions: overall form, multi-angle continuity, use,
controls, CMF, component relationships, service or meaningful variants. Develop
important conclusions into readable views; a hero alone may not explain the object.
Quantity follows useful coverage and user scope, without a fixed template.

Each image needs purpose, viewpoint, visible features, consistency, acceptance and
adequate size. Carry decisions and unresolved engineering needs into the specification.
Identify future mockups, fit tests, material samples or engineering review. Generated
views do not certify feasibility, compliance, tolerances or manufacturing readiness.
