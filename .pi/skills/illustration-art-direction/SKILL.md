# Illustration and Art Direction

Develop narrative imagery, recurring subjects and visual storytelling systems.

## Selection boundary

Own communication-category decisions about narrative imagery, characters and
visual continuity. In a UX scope, contribute illustrations as supporting knowledge;
the interface primary retains responsibility for flows and controls. Exact captions
or explanatory HTML are presentation support, not this module's core image medium.

## Establish the premise

Define story, audience, medium, emotional tone and what the viewer should notice,
feel and understand. Explore metaphors and viewpoints before selecting surface style.
Extract line, shape, value, texture and composition principles from references,
rather than imitating an artist's complete signature.

For real subjects preserve identifying geometry, silhouette or cultural detail
when recognition matters. Separate documentary features from artistic treatment;
original interpretation need not turn a specific subject into a generic motif.

## Develop the visual language

Compare narrative lenses, composition and material languages in the same application.
Change more than style adjectives. Evaluate recognition, emotional meaning,
readability and series coherence; use deliberate distortion instead of accidental
loss of essential traits.

Specify silhouette, proportion, palette, light, texture and detail hierarchy.
For recurring subjects distinguish invariants from changing pose, expression,
costume and scene. Design focal relationships, depth, crop and adaptations to
small placements or alternate aspect ratios.

## Communicate the series

Choose scenes, expressions, views and variants from actual narrative questions,
without a fixed image count. Carry visible style rules, subject features, reference
roles and composition into self-contained image prompts. Include required literal
lettering in those prompts; use deterministic text authoring where the host supports it, and otherwise retain
exact copy in the specification without claiming image glyph fidelity.
Judge narrative clarity, emotional specificity and identifiable continuity, rather
than trusting a shared style adjective.
