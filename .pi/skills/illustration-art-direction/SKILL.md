# Illustration and Art Direction

Use this independent knowledge module to inform the canonical Design Spec.
Select the dimensions relevant to the brief; the Agent contract governs tools,
output formats, image-size ceilings and stage completion.

## Visual premise
Define the story, audience, medium, emotional tone and role of the illustration.
Explore visual metaphors and narrative viewpoints before selecting a surface
style. Establish what the viewer should notice, feel and understand.

Use references to identify principles of line, shape, value, texture and
composition. Develop an original treatment rather than requesting imitation
of a particular artist's complete signature.
When depicting a real subject, also study its identifying features. An original
illustration can preserve landmark geometry, product silhouette or cultural
detail without copying the source photograph's composition. Distinguish
documentary features from artistic treatment; do not flatten the subject into
a generic motif if the brief needs recognition.

## Art direction

Explore different narrative lenses, visual metaphors, composition and material
languages around the same story. Decompose references into useful relations
of shape, value, rhythm and texture; synthesize a treatment with its own purpose.
Change more than rendering adjectives when offering alternate directions.
Compare subject recognition, emotional meaning, readability and series coherence
in the same application. Develop a reproducible shape/detail hierarchy and
identify exact-subject, pose and production dependencies; choose deliberate
expressive distortion rather than accidental loss of essential traits.
Define silhouette, proportion, shape language, palette roles, light, texture
and detail density. Relate those decisions to character, atmosphere and
readability. For recurring subjects, specify invariant traits and the traits
that change across expressions, poses, costumes or scenes.

Design subject-background relationships, depth, focal hierarchy and crop.
Consider how the artwork adapts to small placements, alternate aspect ratios
or sequences when the brief requires them.

## Coverage and handoff
Select scenes, character views, expressions, environmental studies or visual
variants according to the narrative questions. There is no fixed image count;
a character system may need several distinct views, while a single editorial
illustration may be sufficient.

Carry observable style rules, recurring subject descriptions, reference roles
and composition into execution-ready Design Spec prompts. Keep exact lettering
as separate structured content for deterministic presentation.

## Quality judgment
Evaluate narrative clarity, emotional specificity and consistency without
suppressing unusual viewpoints or expressive distortion. Do not claim a
character's continuity solely from a shared style adjective; specify the
actual silhouette and identifying details.
