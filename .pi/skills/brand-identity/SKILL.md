# Brand Identity

Use this optional module for new identities, rebrands, brand extensions, and identity-system concepts.

## Position Before Style

Define the identity's strategic center:

- audience, category, context, and competitive difference;
- purpose, promise, personality, values, and desired perception;
- cultural meaning, linguistic implications, and sensitivity risks;
- existing equity, protected assets, and elements that may change;
- practical touchpoints and conditions in which the identity must work.

Strategy should constrain and inspire the visual idea, not become a decorative narrative added afterward.

## Subject, Source and Existing Identity

Identify what the brand represents before deciding how to stylize it. For a
place, institution, cultural subject or product brand, distinguish:

- the actual subject and identifying features documented by research;
- source photos or illustrations used to understand it;
- existing identity assets that may need preservation, authorized redesign or
  exclusion from the new identity.

A recognizable new drawing of the subject is not the same design operation as
copying its photograph or an existing logo. Unknown source-image permissions do
not by themselves require removing the subject. Record applicable asset-specific
conditions and unresolved questions; do not infer a project-wide ban.
Study existing identities and source images to understand recognition, local
context, visual relationships and design opportunities. Do not prohibit viewing
an official mark merely because the new identity must be original. Extract and
transform relevant principles rather than tracing distinctive marks or copying
another design. Viewing for research, uploading pixels for an approved edit and
placing the original asset in finished work are separate decisions.

Evaluate faithful depiction, selective stylization and abstraction as different
communication choices. Preserve the identifying silhouette, geometry, spatial
relationships, material cues or cultural detail when recognition depends on
them. Abstraction may be useful, but must not erase the intended referent merely
to appear original. Not every brand needs a literal icon; justify the approach.

For a documented five-span landmark bridge, compare treatments that retain the
five-span rhythm with more interpretive alternatives. Choose by recognition,
meaning and suitability, not a presumed requirement to avoid the real bridge.
Do not copy a pre-existing bridge mark or invent unsupported landmark details.

## Professional Development Sequence

For exploratory work, form a concept space from positioning tension, subject
recognition, brand behavior and expression medium. Decompose references into
meaning, silhouette, rhythm, material and application rules; recombine useful
elements around a new thesis rather than borrow a whole identity. Transfer a
principle from another domain only when it advances recognition or experience.
Compare directions on distinct positioning/visual mechanisms, not recolored
versions of one mark. A subject-led symbol, behavior-led identity and
narrative-led system can answer different brand questions without making one
universally superior. Test each in the same real touchpoint. Selection should
explain recognition, differentiated meaning and reproduction tradeoffs; do not
claim independently verified worldwide uniqueness, historical provenance or
official cultural authenticity without evidence. Original metaphor, proposed
symbolism and poetic interpretation are creative choices, not historical facts;
evaluate their relevance and clarity without requiring proof that they already
exist. Distinguish a proposed cultural reading from a documented tradition.

1. Establish audience, positioning, subject, user-requested style and application
   conditions. Separate confirmed requirements from research opportunities.
2. Inspect subject references and extract the features that make this subject
   distinct from generic category imagery. Record source ids and observations.
3. Explore concept hypotheses through different meaning, composition, mark and
   verbal strategies. Do not make every alternative a variation of the same
   arbitrary abstract line. Compare recognition, distinction and cultural fit.
4. Develop the selected mark or identity device through silhouette, positive/
   negative space, proportion, typography relationship, optical balance and
   scale. Preserve essential subject features; simplify secondary detail only
   when the intended reading survives. Define full-detail and small-use versions
   when necessary, rather than simplifying every application to the smallest one.
5. Extend the idea into color roles, type, grids, imagery, materials and verbal
   behavior. Specify what stays consistent and what adapts by medium.
6. Plan applications in the actual requested contexts: signs in their setting,
   campaign imagery with meaningful subjects, packaging with tangible use and
   digital/public touchpoints where relevant. A mark board is not an application.
7. Review recognition, legibility at intended size, cultural accuracy, coherence,
   contrast, practical reproduction and distinctiveness from existing identities.
   Express selected features in concrete prompts; do not ban them in negatives.

This sequence supports reasoning, not a mandatory tool-call or document loop.
Designer specifies the proposal; Builder executes it.

Compare serious directions in a shared diagnostic touchpoint before committing:
how does each identify this specific subject, communicate its promise and behave
under the same content/use conditions? Give each comparable substantive detail;
do not reject a subject-led direction merely because a behavior-led one is easier
to extend. Explain recognition and expressive tradeoffs alongside scalability.
Use written studies or planned comparison imagery as appropriate, not compulsory
generation of every candidate. Keep proposed styles distinct from user preferences.

## Identity Dimensions

Mission, behavior, and visual identity can be used as a reasoning lens, but they are not mandatory document sections:

- mission captures what the brand stands for and why it matters;
- behavior describes how the brand acts, speaks, and creates trust;
- visual identity translates those principles into a recognizable system.

For an extension, preserve established equity. For a rebrand, explain what is retained and what changes. For a speculative identity, distinguish hypotheses from researched facts.

## System Decisions

Create a coherent identity logic rather than a collection of assets. Address the decisions relevant to the brief:

- identity thesis and verbal character;
- mark, wordmark, symbol, or lockup behavior;
- palette roles and contrast intent;
- typography roles and hierarchy;
- grid, shape language, motif, imagery, or motion principles;
- consistency anchors and prohibited treatments;
- application coverage that tests recognition, flexibility and actual use.

Document explicit preservation instructions and evidenced conditions for each
existing logo, wording, symbol or source asset. Distinguish a new identity from
authorized redesign and exact preservation. Do not silently transform assets
required to remain exact or imply official affiliation for a speculative brand.
These asset rules do not automatically apply to the real object depicted.

## Cultural Judgment

Check symbolism, color meaning, naming, gesture, historical associations, and accessibility in the intended markets. Prefer evidence-backed interpretation and clearly label uncertain readings.

## Proposal Coverage

Let the brief and communication questions determine the artifact set without
a preset image count. Plan enough distinct views to explain the identity and
its requested applications.
Explore applicable alternatives, mark/wordmark relationships, lockups, type and
motif details, campaign formats, environmental/signage scenes, digital and print
touchpoints, packaging or merchandise and relevant variation. Make significant
design decisions visible in dedicated outputs rather than hiding the complete
system in one small identity board. This is a task-dependent exploration map,
not a mandatory fixed list or an upper bound on deliverables.
Each image states its purpose, subject features, applicable asset conditions,
consistency anchors, acceptance criteria, aspect
ratio and output size. Do not execute generation from this Skill.
Design the brand name, tagline and application-specific copy alongside the
visual identity. Include exact strings and their typographic roles in each
applicable image prompt; a brand application should not default to an unnamed
graphic or blank mockup. Preserve user-approved names and avoid invented official
claims. Choose meaningful copy and legible hierarchy rather than decorative
pseudo-text. Generated typography is allowed; do not impose a textless policy.

## Quality Lens

A strong identity is distinctive, appropriate, coherent, memorable, adaptable, and operational. Creativity is encouraged when it strengthens positioning and recognition instead of producing disconnected novelty.
