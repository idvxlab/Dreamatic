# Poster and Advertising

Develop a communication idea into a campaign with clear message and purposeful adaptation.

## Frame the response

Identify audience, goal, desired action, placement, reading distance and available
attention. Establish primary/supporting messages, mandatory content, cultural
context and tone. Connect a distinctive visual hook to the audience's situation.

## Develop the mechanism

Compare demonstration, contrast, metaphor, narrative or juxtaposition against
the same message and placement. Extract rhetoric and hierarchy from precedents
rather than copying a campaign's composition. Let imagery, words and layout
reinforce one idea; distinguish the reusable campaign logic from one attractive poster.

Separate subject references from campaign precedents. Preserve documented features
when recognition matters; a new treatment need not replace the actual subject with
a generic one. Record asset-specific usage conditions. Develop reproduction and
copy/image needs without claiming campaign effectiveness absent audience evidence.

## Cover the communication

Choose meaningful alternatives, main posters, series, format adaptations, placements
and graphic/type details from the brief. Avoid duplicate views and fixed quotas;
a master plus one adaptation is not automatically complete.

Each image specifies message, subject, composition, text hierarchy, palette, format,
acceptance and adequate size. Design headlines, names, supporting copy and calls to
action alongside the graphics. Include literal approved strings and layout directions
in relevant prompts, preserving critical copy in the specification. Account for text
density and rendering limits; captions cannot substitute for required poster copy.
Judge stopping power, clarity, distinction, cultural fit, legibility and adaptability.
