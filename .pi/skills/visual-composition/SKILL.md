# Visual Composition

Organize attention and reading across graphics, pages and presentation imagery.

## Develop the reading path

Compare focal relationships, density, positive/negative space and reading order
using the same content. Extract perceptual relationships from precedents rather
than adopting a familiar template. Evaluate at the intended format and distance;
revise the composition mechanism when feedback rejects it, not just its colors.

Give the primary idea a dominant signal and group support. Hierarchy can come
from position, contrast, spacing, direction and depth as well as size.

## Resolve the relationships

- Grid: fit content and format; establish deliberate margins, alignment and exceptions.
- Spacing: keep a recognizable rhythm while adapting density to information and distance.
- Typography: assign roles and tune line length, leading, tracking and contrast.
- Color: use luminance and background relationships for legibility and emphasis.

Avoid accidental near-alignments, competing focal elements and unnecessary nested
layout systems. Dense compositions should still expose grouping and sequence.
Expressive asymmetry or tension is valid when purposeful.

## Adapt across formats

Identify invariants and reflow rules. Preserve the focal idea, hierarchy, identity,
protected assets and text zones rather than relying on crops of a master composition.
Treat exact wording and generated lettering according to the actual medium.
Judge clarity, rhythm, balance, intentionality and adaptability together.
