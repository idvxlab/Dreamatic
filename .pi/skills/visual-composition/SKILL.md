# Visual Composition

Use this optional module when layout and visual organization materially affect the concept. It supports posters, boards, presentations, interfaces, editorial layouts, and composed showcase imagery.

## Hierarchy

Make the intended reading order explicit. Give the primary idea a dominant signal, group supporting information, and reduce elements that compete without adding meaning. Hierarchy may come from scale, position, contrast, spacing, direction, or depth rather than size alone.

## Grid and Alignment

Choose a grid that fits the content and format. Align related elements, establish deliberate margins, and let exceptions carry meaning. Avoid accidental near-alignments and unnecessary layout systems within one composition.

## Spacing and Density

Use a consistent spacing rhythm while adapting density to viewing distance and information load. Preserve breathing room around focal elements. Dense areas should still reveal grouping and sequence.

## Typography

Assign clear roles for display, heading, body, label, and data text as needed. Control line length, leading, tracking, contrast, and alignment. Treat generated typography cautiously when exact wording or legibility is critical.

## Contrast and Color

Use contrast to express hierarchy and maintain legibility. Check luminance, scale, texture, and background interaction. Color roles should support the design system rather than multiply without purpose.

## Cross-Format Behavior

Identify which relationships must remain fixed and which may reflow across aspect ratios. Preserve the focal idea, hierarchy, consistency anchors, protected assets, and safe text zones. Do not assume that cropping a master composition is an adequate adaptation.

## Quality Lens

Judge the composition by clarity, balance, rhythm, emphasis, legibility, intentionality, and adaptability. Creative asymmetry or controlled tension is valid when it serves the communication goal.
