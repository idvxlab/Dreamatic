# Evidence for design decisions

Begin with the decisions the brief needs to resolve. A useful research question
changes a design choice: audience/use constraints, subject recognition, material
behavior, communication mechanism, spatial organization or an interaction model.
Separate supplied facts, external evidence, observations and proposed interpretation.

## Select and assess evidence

Prefer identifiable primary sources for factual claims and original project sources
for precedents. Explain what each source can establish, how closely it matches the
use context, and what remains unknown. Popularity or an attractive render is not
proof of usability, engineering readiness or suitability for this brief.

Compare independent evidence where consequences or disagreement justify it.
When sources conflict, retain their context, date, method and limitations rather
than averaging incompatible claims. Distinguish a source not found from a source
that contradicts a proposal. Search snippets and inaccessible leads are not a
substitute for inspected evidence; use only the reading/search capabilities present.

## Extract transferable methods

Describe which relationship makes a precedent work: hierarchy, geometry, sequence,
use, construction, material, light or feedback. Explain what can transfer and why,
and what belongs specifically to the original context. A precedent is not a layout
to copy wholesale or proof that a new proposal has already been validated.

Inspect visual details only when the actual image and a suitable inspection
capability are available. Filenames and captions can explain intended content but
do not prove observed geometry, legibility or quality.

## Hand off usable conclusions

Connect findings to concrete opportunities, constraints and open questions for the
owning design discipline. Preserve source identity and supplied citation numbering;
the host owns report formatting and delivery. Mark unsupported assumptions and the
future observation, prototype or specialist verification needed to resolve them.
Do not fabricate interviews, personas, measurements, permissions or study results.
Stop when additional research would not materially change the relevant decisions.
