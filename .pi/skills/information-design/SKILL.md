# Information and Data Visualization Design

Choose representations that answer the reader's question without distorting evidence.

## Selection boundary

Own communication-category explanatory graphics, or a UX scope centered on data
inspection and interpretation. A dashboard's truthful encodings can be the primary
design problem; unrelated application workflows still require task-interaction
knowledge. Multi-category eligibility expresses these specific responsibilities,
not an ability to own every interface task.

## Establish evidence and encoding

Identify audience, comparison, source, units, period, denominators, missing values
and uncertainty. Separate measured data, derivations, examples and speculation.
Choose comparison, trend, distribution, part-to-whole, spatial, process or network
encodings from the relationship rather than decorative chart variety.

Compare plausible representations against the same evidence. Define geometry,
ordering, channels and annotations; test what readers can infer and which mistaken
comparison to prevent. Novel metaphors must preserve the meaning of the data.

## Preserve truthful reading

Use interpretable scales and baselines. Explain truncation, aggregation and
normalization. Distinguish correlation from causation and avoid unwarranted precision.
Specify labels, legends, units, reading order and color-independent distinctions.
Simplify without deleting interpretive context; flag gaps instead of inventing values.

## Deliver the representation

Keep exact data, copy, geometry and encoding rules in the specification. Relevant
image prompts include literal titles, labels and legends; captions supplement these
rather than replace them. Where deterministic authoring is available, use it for exact quantitative geometry.
Otherwise preserve exact data and encoding rules in the specification and treat
generated views as concepts, not verified quantitative graphics. Generated illustrations do not establish verified
data accuracy or exact lettering.

Choose overviews, details and comparisons by distinct reader questions. Judge
comprehension, honest inference, uncertainty and maintenance complexity, not visual
attractiveness alone.
